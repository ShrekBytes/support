Jump to a moment: the ingredient check, the step picture and the doneness check are the three things that make this a cooking app rather than a checklist. The shopping list sits next to them and never writes back — ticking an item off in the shop does not tick it off the recipe.

Philosophy — kept, re-voiced

Kept: one step at a time · time is the core · write once, reuse · quiet surface, one colour per dish · never block on a notification · honest states · one dish at a time.

Added because cooking needs it: heat is a second axis · a clock lies, so every timed step ships a doneness test · hands are wet, so voice + screen-awake + big targets · one dish may hold many pans · servings change, so every quantity re-computes in fractions.

The other repo

Recipes live in their own GitHub repo. The app reads an index.json and downloads .recipe.json files. This is the exact format it expects:

oncue-recipes/
  index.json
  recipes/
    hokkaido-ramen.recipe.json
{
  "schema": "oncue.recipe/1",
  "id": "hokkaido-ramen",
  "name": "Hokkaido Ramen",
  "author": "Aiko Tanaka",
  "colour": "peach",
  "food": "#C96A33",
  "servings": 2,
  "tools": ["Dutch oven", "Fine sieve"],
  "ingredients": [{
    "group": "Broth",
    "q": 500, "u": "ml",
    "n": "pork bone broth",
    "sub": "2 instant packets"
  }],
  "steps": [{
    "t": "Caramelise the onions",
    "b": "Low and slow, no rushing.",
    "min": 12,
    "heat": "low",
    "art": "pan",
    "cue": {
      "checks": ["Deep amber, not brown"]
    }
  }]
}
